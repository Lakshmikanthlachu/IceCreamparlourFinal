BEHIND THE SCENES · OCT 12, 2024

The Science of Churning:
Ultra-Creamy Gelato

It isn’t luck. It’s temperature control, butterfat ratio, and a slow-churn method most factories skip entirely.

Traditional ice cream is churned fast and frozen hard, pumping in air to cut costs — often 50% or more of the final volume. Gelato is different: we churn slowly, at a lower speed, which keeps overrun down to roughly 20-30%. Less air means a denser, silkier bite in every scoop.

We also serve gelato slightly warmer than typical ice cream — around -12°C rather than -18°C. At that temperature the fat crystals stay small and the texture stays soft-scoopable, so the flavor actually reaches your tongue instead of hiding behind a wall of ice crystals.

Every batch is churned in small 8-liter drums, never more, so the cream never sits waiting and every tub reaches the case within hours of finishing.

It’s All in the Overrun.
